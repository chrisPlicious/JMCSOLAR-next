How the hybrid inverter decides

  1. Solar powers the home first. Every watt used on the spot is a watt you don't buy.
  2. Extra solar charges the battery, up to its 5 kW charging limit.
  3. Then it goes to the grid for net-metering credit. Without net metering, the inverter holds the panels back instead.
  4. When solar isn't enough, the battery steps in at up to 5 kW — but it keeps a 20% reserve for brownouts.
  5. The grid covers whatever is left.
  6. In a brownout the inverter cuts off from the grid and runs your home on solar and battery, down to a 5% cut-off.

Prototype notes — what's different from the reference video

  • Laid out as a single-line wiring diagram. Right-angle wires, with every flow passing through the hybrid inverter as in a real installation.
  • Net meter between the inverter and the grid. Its display shows energy bought (+) or sold back (−), and the peso billing or credit per hour.
  • Realistic battery. It delivers its full 5 kW until it reaches its reserve, rather than output shrinking with charge level.
  • Spare solar charges the battery first, then exports. The reference never charged the battery.
  • Brownout switch. The home stays on from solar and battery — the main reason to choose hybrid over on-grid.
  • Net metering switch. Shows export credit versus solar going unused.
  • Dots are tied to live flows. When a flow stops, its dots fade out at once — no stray dots heading to the grid.
  • Controls wrap and stack, so nothing scrolls sideways on a phone. The diagram re-arranges into a tall layout on phones.
  • Peso impact and a plain-language sentence explain the current state (the sentence is also announced to screen readers). Sliders work with the keyboard, and motion is replaced by static arrows for reduced-motion users.
  • Play a day runs 24 hours in 40 seconds, including a 6–8 PM brownout, with the battery charging and draining over time.
