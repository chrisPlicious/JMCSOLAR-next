How a grid-tie system decides

  1. Solar powers the home first. Every watt used on the spot is a watt you don't buy.
  2. Extra solar flows out through the net meter and earns credit on your bill. Without net metering, the inverter holds the panels back instead.
  3. When solar isn't enough, the grid fills the gap instantly — no battery needed.
  4. At night the grid supplies everything.
  5. In a brownout the inverter switches off. This anti-islanding rule protects line workers, so the home loses power even in full sun.

Prototype notes

  • Same diagram and engine as the hybrid simulator, with the battery removed and a grid-tie inverter in the middle.
  • Net meter shows energy bought (+) or sold back (−), with the peso billing or credit per hour.
  • Brownout shows anti-islanding: the inverter switches off and the home goes dark even in full sun — the clearest way to show why people upgrade to hybrid.
  • Solar used at home shows how much of the solar output your home uses directly, versus exporting it.
  • Play a day runs 24 hours in 40 seconds, including a 1:00–2:30 PM brownout.
